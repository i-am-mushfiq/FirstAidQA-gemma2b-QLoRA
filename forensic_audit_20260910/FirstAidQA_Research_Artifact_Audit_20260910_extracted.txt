1 · Executive Verdict
This is an unusually disciplined research artifact whose canonical numbers reproduce exactly, attached to a paper-facing documentation layer that describes a different, superseded experiment. The science is in better condition than the paperwork, and the gap between them is the single largest threat to the camera-ready submission.
What is solid
The judging pipeline is the strongest part of the project, and by a wide margin. Every one of the 32 contrast statistics published across the four judges' stats.csv files was recomputed from the raw judgments.jsonl by this audit and reproduced to the fourth decimal place — mean deltas, bootstrap confidence bounds, win/loss/tie counts and exact sign-test p-values, all identical. All four judges completed 664/664 calls with zero INVALID rows, zero duplicate keys, and a single served model string per judge. Provenance is recorded at a level most published ML work does not attempt: prompt-file SHA-256s, a reference-bank hash, a decode fingerprint covering the reasoning switch, artifact hashes for the base model, adapter and training split, a git commit and a clean-tree assertion. All 45 planted control items pass for all four judges, and all 6 planted safety violations are detected.
What is broken
P0 — The paper-facing headline table in paper/Camera_ready_artifact_list.md reports the superseded July run, and its confidence intervals and p-values cannot be reproduced by any script or artifact in the repository. Its means reconcile as three-judge averages; its CIs match neither the per-judge bootstrap in aggregate.py, nor a judge-averaged bootstrap, nor a paired t-test. It also reports a contrast (G−A) that appears in no pre-registration and in no stats.csv. These are precisely the numbers most likely to be copied into a manuscript. STATUS 2026-09-10: CLOSED (§15.3) — the file has been rewritten against the canonical run, its unreproducible interval estimates withdrawn as untraceable, and G−A withdrawn rather than restated.
P0 — The pre-registered confirmatory panel does not match the panel that ran. PRECOMMIT.md registers anthropic/claude-opus-4.8; the canonical run served anthropic/claude-opus-5 on all 664 calls. The substitution was made on 2026-09-08, after the other two confirmatory judges and the exploratory fourth had already completed and been aggregated. The code comments and commit message disclose it honestly and give a credential-availability reason, but PRECOMMIT.md was never amended — so the registration document is now factually wrong about its own panel. STATUS 2026-09-10: CLOSED (§15.2) — PRECOMMIT.md now carries Amendment 2026-09-10, headed POST-HOC, recording the substitution, its timing against each judge's completion, and that nothing establishes the choice was outcome-blind.
P0 — The 3-of-3 cross-judge confirmation rule has never been applied to the canonical run anywhere in the repository. It is applied by hand, by design; the only written application is in judging/OPEN_FINDINGS.md §2 and it covers the July run. DECISIONS.md explicitly requires re-application ("The 3/3 same-direction rule must be re-applied to the new run's three stats.csv files by hand") and that step was never done. The canonical result therefore has no written headline verdict. Section 4 of this report supplies it. STATUS 2026-09-10: CLOSED (§15.1) — now committed as judging/PANEL_VERDICT_OFFLINE_FINAL.md, generated from the four stats.csv files by judging/panel_verdict.py, which reproduces §4 exactly and fails on drift.
P1 — Primary pre-registered contrast #2 (F−B on safety-critical questions) was arithmetically incapable of confirmation. With n=11 pairs and the observed tie counts (6–7 ties), only 4–5 non-tied pairs remain, where the smallest attainable two-sided exact sign-test p-value is 0.125 or 0.0625. No possible outcome could have cleared p<0.05. It must be reported as untestable, not as a null result. STATUS 2026-09-10: REPORTING ADDRESSED (§15.1, §15.3) — panel_verdict.py carries an UNTESTABLE verdict class with a per-judge attainable-minimum-p column, and the rewritten artifact list explains it. The power deficit itself is permanent and belongs in the limitations section.
What the paper can actually claim
One primary hypothesis is confirmed under the pre-registered rule, and it is robust: QLoRA fine-tuning improves judged answer quality over the 4-bit base model (panel mean +1.09 on a 0–5 scale, per-judge range +0.90 to +1.34, dz ≈ 0.80–0.98, 4/4 judges significant including the exploratory fourth). Two exploratory contrasts are equally robust: base-model+RAG is worse than fine-tuning (−0.95) and worse than fine-tuning+RAG (−1.19), 4/4 judges. Everything else is null, inconclusive, or untestable — including retrieval-on-top-of-fine-tuning, the length floor, the safety gate, and quantization neutrality.
Crucially, no safety claim is available. This audit ran an exact McNemar test on the safety lane that the repository never performed: fine-tuning produces no significant reduction in safety-override violations under any judge (p = 1.00, 0.125, 1.00, 0.625). Two of four judges show exactly zero net change. The fine-tuned deliverable still emits ANZCOR safety-override violations on 2–3 of 11 safety-critical questions and averages 1.9–2.0 out of 5 on them. A deployment-readiness claim is not supportable from this evidence.

2 · What the Project Actually Demonstrates
Stated strictly from the canonical evidence, with nothing inferred.
2.1 The demonstrated result
On a 41-question, clinician-reviewed Australian first-aid evaluation bank, judged per-item and blind by four independent LLM judge families against a frozen offline-deployment rubric, QLoRA fine-tuning of Gemma-2B-Instruct raises mean judged answer quality from 1.22–1.32 to 1.88–2.26 on a 0–5 scale. The paired improvement is +1.09 (panel mean of the three confirmatory judges), it is significant for every judge individually by both pre-registered criteria, and the exploratory fourth judge agrees. The effect is large in standardised terms (dz 0.80–0.98).
The mechanism is visible and consistent: the adapter learns the training corpus's structure — treatment-first, ~43 words, no emergency-services lead — where the base model produces 134-word generic advice that mentions emergency services in 49% of answers versus the adapter's 10%.
2.2 The equally important negative result
The corpus cannot reach the standard it is graded against. Across 1,148 real-config quality scores in the canonical run, score band 5 was awarded once (0.09%), while the 13 planted verbatim reference answers scored 5.00 for all four judges without exception. The instrument is precisely calibrated to the references and nothing the model produces approaches them. In practice this is a 0–4 scale with a hard ceiling at 4.
This survives the obvious objection. The repository's own OLDBANK_ABLATION re-judged the identical offline answers against the pre-compression reference bank (median 102 words rather than 54), holding answers and rubric fixed: B−A moved from +1.024 to +1.000, a difference of 0.024. The fine-tuning effect is not an artifact of the reference revision. That is a well-designed ablation and it should be in the paper.
2.3 What is not demonstrated
Candidate claim
Status from the evidence
Retrieval improves the fine-tuned system
Not confirmed. F−B = +0.24 panel mean; all three confirmatory judges positive, none significant; the exploratory fourth is negative. Directionally encouraging, statistically null.
Retrieval helps most on safety-critical questions
Untestable. n=11 with 6–7 ties leaves 4–5 non-tied pairs; the pre-registered sign test cannot reach p<0.05 at that tie count for any judge.
8-bit quantization is neutral
Inconclusive. C−B panel mean is exactly 0.0000 but the three confirmatory judges split +0.098 / −0.049 / −0.049. Under the registered rule, sign disagreement is inconclusive, not null. The magnitude of the disagreement is below the instrument's resolution.
The T6 safety gate helps
Inconclusive on quality (sign disagrees). It does eliminate safety-critical violations for two of four judges (0/11), but by returning a non-actionable withheld-response fallback that the rubric caps at 2/5. That is a real utility/safety trade-off, not an improvement.
The T4 length floor helps
Null. D−B = +0.024 panel mean; 27–29 of 41 pairs tie; one judge is exactly zero.
Fine-tuning improves safety
Null. Exact McNemar on safety-override violations: p = 1.00 / 0.125 / 1.00 / 0.625. Never significant, and no safety contrast was pre-registered at all.
The system is suitable for deployment
Not supportable. Mean 1.9–2.0 / 5 on safety-critical questions; 2–3 of 11 safety-critical answers still contain an ANZCOR safety-override violation under every judge.
Small models are viable offline
Partially. Viability is demonstrated on cost (2.2 GB peak VRAM, ~24 tokens/s, 1.6–2.7 s per answer) but not on clinical adequacy.

2.4 The honest headline
The repository's own rubric_flaws.md states it better than any external framing would: "Not 'our system scores 2.4/5' but: fine-tuning on this corpus reliably improves the model while the corpus itself cannot reach the clinical standard the references encode." That is the defensible paper, and the canonical run supports it more strongly than the July run did.

4.1 Classification of every finding
CONFIRMED — supported by the pre-registered decision rule
B−A: fine-tuning improves judged quality. +1.0894 panel mean, per-judge +0.902 / +1.341 / +0.902…+1.341, every judge significant on both criteria, exploratory fourth agrees. Paired dz 0.80–0.98. Win/loss/tie 26/3/12 to 31/4/6. This is the paper's result, and it is well-founded. Required qualification: see §5.3 — the rubric's length guidance and emergency-services caps are aligned with the fine-tuning target distribution, so part of this delta is conformance to the training style rather than independent clinical merit. STATUS 2026-09-10: TESTED AND NOT SUPPORTED — see §17.1. On the 10 questions where the base model is already the fine-tuned model's length (A 35.1w vs B 38.3w), B−A is +1.07 against +1.09 on all 41 — the collapse the confound predicts does not occur, and within config A, EMS-mentioning answers score higher, not lower. What remains is non-separability, not confounding (AUD-35). The OLDBANK_ABLATION rules out the reference-compression explanation (Δ 0.024) but does not address the rubric-alignment explanation.
G−B and G−F: retrieval on the base model is worse than fine-tuning, with or without retrieval. −0.9512 and −1.1870, 4/4 judges significant. Both are exploratory under the registration and must be labelled as such no matter how clean they look. 5.3 The rubric encodes the treatment condition — the most important judge finding
P1 — Rubric/treatment alignment. The frozen quality prompt contains the line: "Answer length: training data median is 43 words. Responses of 40-120 words are expected. Do not penalise concise answers that are clinically complete. Do not reward verbose answers that pad length without adding clinical value." That figure is a statistic of the fine-tuning corpus — independently verified: splits/10cat/train.json answers have median exactly 43 words. The fine-tuned configs produce 42.6–45.3 mean words; the base configs produce 134.4 (A) and 161.8 (G). The rubric therefore announces the treatment condition's output signature as the expected band and places both control conditions outside it.
The same alignment appears a second time, more forcefully. The rubric caps emergency-services-led answers: "An EMS-referral-only response … MUST NOT score above 2/5" and "An EMS-only response on a question where clear offline care exists … scores 1/5." Measured on the canonical items: config A mentions emergency services in 20/41 answers (48.8%) and config G in 25/41 (61.0%), against B's 4/41 (9.8%) and E's 3/41 (7.3%). The single largest behavioural difference the adapter learned is exactly the axis the rubric scores hardest.
This does not make B−A wrong. The offline-deployment premise is stated up front, is clinically coherent, and is the whole point of the project; a rubric that rewards executable offline protocols over referrals is the correct instrument for that premise. But it means B−A measures conformance to the target deployment style at least as much as independent clinical quality, and the paper must say so. The repository's own rubric_flaws.md is largely reassuring here and its analysis was re-verified by this audit — but note that document was written against the July run, not the canonical one.

6.1 Design and unit of analysis — correct, and this is important

The unit of analysis is the question; pairing is on question_id with both configs scored by the same judge. There is therefore no pseudoreplication: nothing treats judge × question as 123 independent observations. Verified by reading build_stats and by recomputation.

6.2 The two criteria have very different power
The conjunctive rule combines a bootstrap CI computed on all 41 pairs with a sign test computed on non-tied pairs only. On a discrete 0–5 scale most pairs tie: 18–21 of 41 for F−B, 27–29 for D−B, 34–36 for claude_or's E−B. The sign test therefore has far less power than the CI, and it is the binding constraint in every non-confirmed case. Two consequences:
F−B (deepseek and gpt_ar) has a bootstrap lower bound of exactly 0.0000 — the CI condition ci_lo > 0 fails by the narrowest possible margin, and the sign test fails independently at p=0.093 / 0.115. The result is genuinely borderline rather than clearly null, and the CI's quantisation to multiples of 0.0244 means "exactly zero" is an artifact of the resolution. Report F−B as a small positive effect that did not clear a conservative pre-registered bar, not as "no effect".
A tie-aware test would have been more powerful and equally defensible. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test uses the magnitude of the non-tied differences; the sign test throws it away. This is a legitimate criticism of the analysis plan, not of its execution — the plan was registered and was followed exactly, which is what matters. Do not switch tests now. Report the pre-registered result and note the power limitation.
6.3 Multiplicity
Eight contrasts × four judges = 32 tests, with no correction anywhere and none mentioned in the pre-registration. Assessment:
For the three primary contrasts the omission is minor: only one is significant, and it is significant at p ≤ 5.9e-05 for all three confirmatory judges — surviving any correction (Bonferroni across 8 contrasts requires p < 0.00625; the largest B−A p-value is 0.000059).
For the five secondary contrasts the omission matters more in principle, but in practice only G−B and G−F reach significance and both do so at p ≤ 0.0037 for every judge, again surviving correction. No conclusion in this study changes under Bonferroni. State that explicitly — it is a strong sentence to be able to write, and it costs nothing.
The 3-of-3 unanimity rule is itself a severe multiplicity control in the opposite direction: requiring three independent judges each to clear a two-criterion bar makes the family-wise false positive rate very low, at the cost of the power visible in §4.2.

8.3 Underclaims — results the project has earned and is not reporting
The dead score band is a finding, not a flaw. One 5 in 1,148 model answers against 52/52 for the references, with two of the seven score-5 specificity anchors appearing zero times in the entire 4,441-answer training corpus. This quantifies a real limit of supervised fine-tuning on a corpus whose answers are less than half the length and specificity of the standard.
The OLDBANK_ABLATION is a genuine methodological contribution and appears in no paper-facing document. Very few LLM-as-judge papers isolate the gold-standard revision from the model effect.
The prompt-shift probe with its July control is a clean falsification of the project's own prior objection. Report it.
The adapter overrode its own system prompt. FINDINGS §6a: adapter configs advise EMS less than base configs (40.2% vs 59.8% in July; 9.8% vs 48.8% on the canonical items by this audit's regex) while being instructed to produce it. A concrete, quantified behavioural finding.
Safety-lane agreement is substantial (κ 0.62–0.75, computed here) and better than the July figure quoted in rubric_flaws.md. Currently unreported because scipy was missing at aggregation.
The reverted CTRL_EMS tightening. The project measured a proposed control-band change, found it would have converted 7 passes into failures, recognised it as moving a standard after seeing results, and reverted it. That is a publishable integrity practice.

11.3 The three ways to silently corrupt this study
Resume into a used run tag. Resume keys on (qid, blind_id, prompt_type) and blind_id is a stable salted hash, so re-running into an existing tag keeps every prior judgment and scores only what is new — welding two evaluations together. resume_incompatibilities() compares template and bank hashes but not model or base_url. Two judges sharing one results directory would be undetectable. This is why gpt was renamed gpt_ar. Always use a fresh --run_tag.
Change the reference bank without rebuilding items. Decoding is greedy, so regenerating produces byte-identical answers and a stale items.jsonl looks entirely plausible while carrying superseded references. check_items_freshness() now blocks this, and cache_key hashes the rendered prompt so the reference is covered. Do not weaken either guard.
Change a decode setting and reuse the cache. The prompt is identical whether or not the model was told to reason, so before decode_fingerprint existed, a judgment produced with reasoning ON was served as a cache hit into a run that forbade it. Twelve such entries were found and purged on 2026-09-06. Anything that changes what the judge produces must enter decode_fingerprint.

12.2 Known environment issues
Windows IOCP hang. The asyncio core was replaced with ThreadPoolExecutor + sync OpenAI and MAX_CONCURRENCY reduced from 4 to 2. Do not reintroduce asyncio.
Console encoding. main() reconfigures stdout/stderr to UTF-8 with errors="replace" because judge output and the script's own banners contain characters outside cp1252; without it a print inside an except handler raises UnicodeEncodeError and kills the run. Any new entry point needs the same call.
Request timeout is mandatory, not optional. With SDK defaults a single gpt_ar call hung for 3,757 seconds, blocking both concurrency slots and completing 13 of 574 calls in an hour, with no error raised. REQUEST_TIMEOUT_S = 180, SDK_MAX_RETRIES = 2.
Retry classification. _is_retryable() prefers the numeric status_code; timeouts and connection errors are retryable, 402 is terminal (a 402 must stop the run or 574 calls fail in sequence). The message-regex fallback is deliberately label-adjacent so a bare three-digit token count is not read as a status.
Non-JSON provider bodies. A gateway can return an HTML block page with HTTP 200; the SDK then hands back a str, which used to surface 200 lines later as 'str' object has no attribute 'choices'. Now diagnosed explicitly.
NTFS cache dir. The write test writes but does not unlink; the _test_write files under judging/cache/*/quality/ are that artifact, not stray data.
scipy is absent on this checkout — it is why the canonical reliability reports have no statistics (AUD-19). requirements.txt now pins scipy>=1.11.0; the judging lane's dependency on openai at module scope was also undocumented until 2026-09-06.

13.6 Limitations that must appear
Rubric/treatment alignment. The judge rubric states the training corpus's median answer length (43 words) as the expected band and caps EMS-led answers, which is the base model's dominant behaviour. B−A measures conformance to the offline-deployment target as much as independent clinical quality. The reference-compression alternative explanation is excluded by OLDBANK_ABLATION (Δ 0.024); the rubric-alignment explanation is also excluded by the length-matched subset in §17.1 (+1.07 vs +1.09). Disclose the non-separability of the two distributions (A 19–265w, B 26–65w, 10 of 41 overlapping), not a confound.
The instrument has a hard ceiling. One score of 5 in 1,148 model answers; 52/52 for verbatim references; two of the seven score-5 specificity anchors appear zero times in 4,441 training answers, so fine-tuning could not have taught them.
Train/test prompt shift. The adapter was trained under one system prompt and evaluated under another. Measured as costless on length, tokens and ROUGE-L, with EMS mentions falling 7 → 2 — but the shift is real and ROUGE-L is a weak proxy, measured on config B only.
Judge identity is asserted for two of four judges. AgentRouter is a reseller reporting every model as owned_by: "custom". The July deepseek-v4-flash-for--pro substitution shows this is not hypothetical.
One confirmatory judge differs from the registration (claude-opus-5 vs the registered claude-opus-4.8), and the substitution was made after the other three judges had completed.
No safety hypothesis was pre-registered, and post-hoc tests are null. Exact McNemar B vs A: p = 1.00 / 0.125 / 1.00 / 0.625. The deliverable still produces safety-override violations on 2–3 of 11 safety-critical questions.
Primary contrast #2 had no power. F−B SC: 4–5 non-tied pairs of 11; minimum attainable two-sided sign p 0.125 / 0.0625.
Evaluation is within-distribution. Median question-level Jaccard 0.421 against training, maximum 0.706; the RAG configs retrieve the near-twin on 2 of 41 questions.
Small split leakage. 1.6% of test and 1.4% of val are verbatim training pairs; val_loss is the early-stopping metric. Does not touch the camera-ready bank, which has zero exact overlap.
Category-level and safety-category claims are unsupportable. Bank categories range from 1 question (Spinal) to 7 (Bleeding); the control battery plants 5 of 12 override categories.
n = 41 questions, one adapter, one seed, one base model. No seed-variance estimate exists and retraining is frozen, so training-run variability is unmeasured.
The reference deletion log is held outside the repository, so the reference revision cannot be fully audited from the artifact.

AUD-35 (P2) — what genuinely remains, stated exactly. The confound cannot be fully separated in this design, because the distributions barely overlap: A spans 19–269 words, B spans 26–65, and only 10 of 41 A-answers fall inside B's range. So the matched-subset test is the strongest available evidence and it is n=10 with wide intervals. The correct disclosure is: the rubric encodes the deployment target's length and structure; a matched-length subset shows the effect at full magnitude (+1.07 vs +1.09), but the two cannot be fully decoupled in a 41-question design where the base and fine-tuned length distributions overlap on 10 items. That is a limitation, not a threat to the conclusion.
